A flight tracker that rides on your disc

discyboi is a 34 mm sensor board that sticks under a disc golf disc. It measures each throw at release and sends the numbers to your browser over Bluetooth: speed, spin, hyzer, nose angle and more.

Open the flight lab Source on GitHub

The lab also works without a disc: enter a throw by hand and watch it fly.

What it measures

Everything is measured on the disc during the throw. No camera, no radar, no net sensors.

Speed

How fast the disc leaves your hand, in m/s, km/h or mph.

Spin

Revolutions per minute at release. A second sensor off-centre keeps reading where a gyro alone tops out, around 670 rpm.

Hyzer

How far the disc is tilted sideways at release, hyzer or anhyzer.

Nose angle

Whether the nose points up or down, and by how much, compared with the flight path.

Launch angle

How steeply the disc is thrown upwards.

Wobble

Off-axis flutter just after release: lower means a cleaner snap.

Forehand or backhand is told apart by the spin direction.

How it works

1

A small board with two motion sensors

A Bluetooth module (nRF54L15) and two motion sensors, one on the spin axis and one 9 mm out. They are read 960 times a second and handle up to 320 g. A CR2032 coin cell powers it.

2

A printed case under the flight plate

The case is 9.6 mm tall and sticks on with tape. A printed jig centres it on the disc's injection mark, so the sensor sits on the spin axis. Board, cell and case add about 12–14 g.

3

Your browser does the rest

Connect from Chrome or Edge on a laptop or Android phone; there is no app to install. Each throw arrives a moment after release and is simulated as a full flight for the disc you pick.

Build one

Everything needed to make one is in the repository.

  • Board: KiCad project, gerbers and a parts list ready to order.
  • Case: OpenSCAD source and printable files for the case and the centring jig.
  • Firmware: Zephyr, built in Docker, updated from the browser over Bluetooth.
  • Web app: TypeScript and three.js, with a small server for accounts and sync.
  • Licence: open hardware and open source. Build your own freely; if you share or sell one, credit the project and share your changes.

Rough single-unit prices from Mouser and JLCPCB, not quotes. A first build also pays for a minimum board order and shipping, about US$60–90 all in. You need to be comfortable reflow-soldering 0.5 mm-pitch parts.
