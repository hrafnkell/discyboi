Disc golf · build it yourself

A flight tracker that rides on your disc

discyboi is a 34 mm sensor board that sticks under a disc golf disc. It measures each throw at release and sends the numbers to your browser over Bluetooth: speed, spin, hyzer, nose angle and more. You throw into a net and get instant readings about your calculated distance. It weighs 10grams with battery and is open source.

Open the flight lab Source on GitHub

The lab also works without a disc: enter a throw by hand and watch it fly.

A hobby project, still being tuned. The angles and speeds have not yet been checked against a radar or camera, so treat them as a really good guide. Everything works, the hardware and software tested with hundreds of real throws. The numbers check out against my tree-tossing spaghetti arm, and the hardware supports pro-level throws handily (in theory).

What it measures

Everything is measured on the disc during the throw. No camera, no radar, no net sensors.

Speed

How fast the disc leaves your hand, in m/s, km/h or mph.

Spin

Revolutions per minute at release. A second sensor off-centre keeps reading where a gyro alone tops out, around 670 rpm.

Hyzer

How far the disc is tilted sideways at release, hyzer or anhyzer.

Nose angle

Whether the nose points up or down, and by how much, compared with the flight path.

Launch angle

How steeply the disc is thrown upwards.

Wobble

Off-axis flutter just after release: lower means a cleaner snap.

Forehand or backhand is told apart by the spin direction.

How it works

1

A small board with two motion sensors

A Bluetooth module (nRF54L15) and two motion sensors, one on the spin axis and one 9 mm out. They are read 960 times a second and handle up to 320 g. A CR2032 coin cell powers it for approx 40hrs of throwing.

2

A printed case under the flight plate

The case is 9.6 mm tall and sticks on with tape. A printed jig helps to center it on the disc, so the sensor sits on the spin axis. Board, cell and case add about 10 g, so sticking it on a lighter disc than you would normally throw would be preferable.

3

Your browser does the rest

Connect from Chrome or Edge on a laptop, desktop or Android phone; there is no app to install. Each throw arrives a moment after release and is simulated as a full flight for the disc you pick. iOS might work with Bluefy or WebBLE but has not been tested.

Want one without the soldering?

I'm gauging interest in having a small batch of boards made. An assembled, tested board would be about US$70, shipped anywhere in the world, or US$10 more with the 3D-printed case and mounting hardware (screws and VHB tape).

Nothing is for sale yet, and signing up doesn't commit you to anything, but please only sign up if you're seriously interested. The boards would be delivered fully assembled, and ready to glue onto your disc and start throwing. Alignment tool included if you get the 3d printed options.
